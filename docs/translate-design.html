Translating pages

Draft for David. Whole pages, on the device with Apple's Translation framework, offered only when a page isn't in one of your languages. Quiet: a mark beside the book, a menu item, nothing that pops up on its own.

Look Page

What Apple's framework can do (tested on this Mac, macOS 27)

Keeping links in placeFrom macOS 26.4 and iOS 26.4 a whole paragraph can be translated with its links and bold marked inside it, and they come back around the right words, even when the order changes ("Ich habe den Bericht gestern gelesen" → "I read the report yesterday"). Code marked "skip" comes back untouched. This is how Firn matches Safari.
Which Macs and iPhonesmacOS 26.4 and later, iOS 26.4 and later. Before that, links can't be kept in place (macOS 15 and 26.0–26.3 could only translate the plain words), so the feature is simply absent there, as Summarize is without Apple Intelligence.
SpeedAbout half a second a paragraph, one at a time; running several at once is no faster. A long page of 60 paragraphs would take about 30 seconds, so Firn translates what's on screen first, then just ahead as you scroll.
Long paragraphsA paragraph of about 1,400 characters or more can make the model repeat itself, so Firn splits long blocks by sentence and checks that what comes back isn't far longer than what went in.
LanguagesAbout 20 languages (47 regional variants), from Arabic to Vietnamese. The list comes from the system, never hard-coded.
First time for a languageApple shows its own sheet to download the language, the first time someone picks it. That's the only network traffic, and the person starts it.

On the Mac: the mark, beside the book

A small translate mark shows in the address bar only on pages that can be offered, in the same quiet place as Reader's book and looking just like it. One click translates; while working it breathes gently; once translated it takes the space's color, as the book does when Reader is on. Click it again for the original. Pointing at it says what it will do.

The tab's title

The tab's name in the sidebar stays as the site wrote it at first (the plan leaves the title out of step 2). Worth deciding: Safari translates the title too.

The wait

No progress bar and no overlay: the page stays readable the whole time. Paragraphs on screen change one after another as each is ready, with a 150ms fade (Reduce Motion: they just change, and the mark stops breathing and simply dims). Paragraphs further down translate as you scroll toward them, so a long page never makes you wait for the end. Text a page adds later (comments, "load more") translates a moment after it appears.

If something goes wrong

If the language download is declined or a paragraph can't be translated, the mark goes back to its plain look and the rest of the page stays as the site wrote it. No alert: pointing at the mark says "Couldn't translate this page".

On the Mac: the View menu and the command bar

In the View menu just under Reader and Summarize, as Safari's sits near its Reader. No shortcut at first (Safari has none); step 4 checks Dia's. The command bar finds it by "translate", the language's name, or the language's own name ("español").

When it's never offered

  • The page is in one of your languages (System Settings › Language & Region), or there's too little text to tell.
  • The page's language isn't one Apple translates, or none of your languages is.
  • macOS before 26.4, or iOS before 26.4.
  • Reader is on (Reader would translate as its own later step), error pages, and Firn's own pages.
